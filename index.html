Mathematical Foundation

Why LCM matters, and why our implementation is provably correct.

GCD Foundation

The Euclidean algorithm computes the greatest common divisor in O(log min(m, n)) time. It is the oldest nontrivial algorithm still in widespread use (Knuth, 1997, p. 335).

LCM Identity

lcm(m, n) = |m × n| gcd(m, n)

Pairwise reduction is associative, so we fold over the array left-to-right without loss of generality (Rosen, 2019, p. 237).

Zero Handling

By convention, lcm(m, 0) = 0. We short-circuit the reduce to avoid division by zero and return the mathematically accepted sentinel value.

Implementation Notes

  • Recursive Euclidean GCD keeps the call stack shallow due to logarithmic depth.
  • Arrow functions and Array.prototype.reduce express the fold idiomatically.
  • Zero short-circuit prevents undefined behavior.
